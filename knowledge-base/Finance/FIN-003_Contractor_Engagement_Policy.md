## Purpose

This policy governs how Contoso Corporation engages, manages, and offboards contractors, consultants, and contingent workers. It establishes classification standards, rate limits, engagement channels, compliance requirements, and the obligations of both the business owner and the contractor.

Misclassification of workers (treating employees as contractors or vice versa) exposes Contoso to significant legal, tax, and regulatory liability. This policy is designed to prevent misclassification and ensure all engagements are structured correctly.

---

## Scope

This policy applies to all engagements of:

- Independent contractors (individuals providing services directly)
- Consulting firms and professional services providers
- Agency-sourced temporary workers
- Statement of Work (SOW) resources from managed service providers
- Freelancers and gig economy workers engaged for defined projects

It does not apply to full-time or part-time employees on fixed-term employment contracts (governed by HR).

## Contractor Classification and IR35 / Worker Misclassification

Before engaging any contractor, the business owner must complete the Worker Classification Assessment in the Procurement portal. This assessment applies the following tests:

| Factor | Employee Signal | Contractor Signal |
|---|---|---|
| Control over work | Contoso directs how work is done | Contractor controls method of delivery |
| Substitution | Person must personally deliver | Contractor can send a substitute |
| Equipment | Uses Contoso equipment | Uses own tools and equipment |
| Financial risk | No financial risk; guaranteed pay | Bears financial risk; can profit or lose |
| Exclusivity | Cannot work for others during engagement | Can work for multiple clients simultaneously |
| Integration | Treated as part of the team; attends all internal meetings | Project-specific engagement; limited integration |
| Duration | Ongoing indefinite engagement | Defined project scope and end date |

If 4 or more employee signals are present, the engagement must be structured as employment, not as a contractor arrangement. Legal and HR must be consulted before proceeding.

## Engagement Duration Limits

| Engagement Type | Maximum Initial Duration | Maximum Total Duration (including extensions) |
|---|---|---|
| Individual contractor | 6 months | 18 months |
| Consulting firm SOW | 12 months per SOW | Renewable; no absolute limit |
| Agency temporary worker | 3 months | 12 months |
| Freelancer (platform) | Per project; maximum 3 months | 6 months per year |

Extensions beyond these limits require:

- Legal review of classification risk
- HR confirmation that the role does not meet the definition of a permanent position
- CFO approval

Contractors who have been engaged for more than 18 cumulative months in a 24-month period must go through a re-evaluation before any further extension.

## Onboarding Requirements

### Before Work Begins

| Requirement | Individual Contractor | Firm / Agency | Freelancer |
|---|---|---|---|
| Signed Contoso contractor agreement | Yes - Legal template | Yes - SOW with Legal-approved terms | Yes - platform agreement |
| Worker Classification Assessment completed | Yes | Yes per named resource | Yes |
| Background check | Required if accessing Restricted data or production systems | Required per resource if accessing Restricted data | Not required |
| IT security onboarding | Required | Required | Required |
| NDAs and IP assignment | Yes - included in standard agreement | Yes - firm NDA; individual IP clauses | Yes |
| System access provisioning | Via IT; limited to what scope requires | Via IT | Via IT; most restrictive access tier |

### Access Provisioning Rules

| System / Data Level | Contractor Access Permitted | Approval Required |
|---|---|---|
| Internal data and standard tools | Yes | Manager and IT |
| Confidential data | Yes, if required for scope | Manager + CISO for individual; IT review for firm |
| Restricted data | Only in exceptional circumstances | VP + CISO + Legal approval |
| Production systems | Requires additional justification | VP + IT Security |
| Source code repositories | Limited scope access; no write to main/production branches | IT Security review |

## Contractor Management Obligations

Business owners engaging contractors are responsible for:

1. Completing the Worker Classification Assessment before engagement starts
2. Ensuring the contractor does not work beyond the agreed scope
3. Not directing the contractor in a way that signals employment (assigning office hours, requiring daily attendance at all-hands meetings, providing equipment without need)
4. Monitoring engagement duration and initiating extension or offboarding processes in a timely manner
5. Reviewing and approving invoices via the Procurement portal within 5 business days of receipt
6. Reporting any concerns about classification, conduct, or scope creep to Procurement and HR immediately

---

## Offboarding

When a contractor engagement ends:

| Step | Action | Owner | Timeline |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Business owner initiates offboarding in Procurement portal | Business owner | 5 days before end date |
| 2 | IT revokes all access and retrieves company equipment | IT | On last day of engagement |
| 3 | Final invoice reviewed and approved | Business owner | Within 5 business days of final delivery |
| 4 | Non-disclosure and IP confirmation | Legal | Within 10 business days |
| 5 | Engagement record closed in Procurement system | Procurement | On completion |

---

## Non-Compliant Engagements

Engagements initiated without the required approvals or worker classification assessment are non-compliant. Consequences include:

- Finance will not process invoices for non-compliant engagements until retroactive approval is obtained
- The business owner is personally accountable for any tax or legal liability arising from misclassification
- Formal disciplinary review under HR-001 for repeated or deliberate non-compliance
